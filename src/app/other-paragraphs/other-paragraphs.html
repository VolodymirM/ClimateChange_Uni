Rising seas, shrinking fresh water

Mapping the Threat: Sea-Level Rise and Saltwater Intrusion

When we map out climate impacts globally, the vulnerabilities of our coastal regions become alarmingly clear. Extensive research published in journals such as Nature Climate Change and Water Resources Research highlights the escalating and irreversible threat of sea-level rise. As oceans thermally expand and polar ice sheets melt, coastal areas face a hidden but devastating consequence: saltwater intrusion. Advanced spatial modelling shows that saltwater is increasingly penetrating freshwater aquifers and agricultural soils. This physical alteration of our land and water resources destroys crop yields and contaminates drinking water. It is the exact scientific mechanism driving the food rationing and permanent water shortages depicted in our futuristic news segment.

The urgent need to protect biodiversity

Ecosystem Collapse and Biodiversity Loss

Furthermore, shifting climate zones and rapid deforestation are driving a global biodiversity crisis. The Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES) Global Assessment Report warns that around one million animal and plant species are currently threatened with extinction. Ecosystems simply cannot adapt quickly enough to the shrinking of their natural habitats and rapidly rising temperatures. This massive loss of life is precisely what contemporary artists, like Camille Henrot in her In the Veins exhibition, strive to communicate visually. Art helps us process the grief of these ecological changes, reminding us that the scientific statistics represent living, breathing ecosystems in distress.

The data is clear, and the environmental models project a challenging future if current trends continue. However, understanding the science is the first crucial step toward mitigation. By recognizing the scale of these environmental shifts, we can better understand the necessity of the sustainable practices and mitigation strategies outlined in our 'Take Action' section.
